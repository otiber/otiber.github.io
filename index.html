How do we build service-based and AI-enabled software whose behaviour can be trusted?

I am a Full Professor at the National School of Artificial Intelligence (ENSIA) in Algiers. My research sits where software engineering, formal methods and artificial intelligence meet.

I began by translating UML models into rewriting logic so they could be model-checked with Maude. My doctoral work on self-healing web service orchestrations led to a long line of research on service similarity, reputation and trust, and later to microservice identification for legacy migration, IoT service discovery and secure AI. Today I work on LLM-based software engineering: neuro-symbolic requirements elicitation, QoS-aware brokering of LLM services, and automated program repair.

Before ENSIA I spent over a decade at the University of Biskra, where I headed the Computer Science department and lead the Services and Software Engineering (2SE) team at the LRP laboratory, with two years at Jouf University in Saudi Arabia.

2008 – 2013

Formal verification of software models. Translating UML state machine and collaboration diagrams into rewriting logic for model checking with Maude.

2012 – 2019

Reliable service-oriented systems. Interface-based similarity of WSDL services (the WSSim tool), finding substitutes to heal failed WS-BPEL orchestrations, and bootstrapping the reputation of newcomer services. Published in JSS and at IEEE ICWS.

2018 – 2023

Service-oriented IoT. Service discovery and device-to-device communication for post-disaster scenarios, and IoT network optimisation (AINA, IWCMC, IEEE ICC, JNCA).

2020 – now

Microservices, distributed transactions and trustworthy AI. Data-centric microservice identification for legacy migration, Maude-based verification of Saga transactions, trust models for service recommendation, medical image steganography and adversarial robustness.

2025 – now

LLM-based and neuro-symbolic software engineering. Using formal verification counterexamples to guide LLM requirements elicitation, SLA-constrained routing of LLM-as-a-Service, and LLM-based program repair. Four doctoral projects started at ENSIA in 2026 extend this to the security of AI-generated code, the robustness and cognition of autonomous LLM agents, and environmentally-aware architecture migration.
